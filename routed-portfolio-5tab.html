The split rule — three questions decide the executor

Is it fully specified with acceptance criteria? No → Copilot (engineer refines in the loop). Yes → next question.
Is the output machine-verifiable — tests/CI as judge? No → Copilot. Yes → next question.
Is it one of many similar subtasks worth parallelizing? No → Copilot coding agent handles the single ticket. Yes → Devin campaign, ACU budget attached.

Governance rail — runs under every stage

Attribute spend per stage and per executor · Copilot credit budgets at cost-center level, overage blocked by default · Devin per-campaign ACU caps, auto-reload off · track cost per merged PR, rework rate, defect escape — a Devin campaign survives only if its cost per merged, verified subtask beats the Copilot-supervised alternative.

Why setup comes first

The flow assumes a prepared portfolio

Tab 1's stage 01 assumes a wiki already exists per app, the human gate assumes a shared artifact convention, and the Devin fork assumes playbooks and ACU budgets are pre-wired. None of that is free — it's a one-time (then continuously refreshed) portfolio investment.

Sequence: index → standardize → tier → wire campaigns → operate

Operating cadence — who runs this

A small platform-enablement squad owns the ecosystem — wiki freshness, template repo, playbook library, MCP endpoints, budgets, dashboards. App teams consume; they don't each rebuild the rails.
Monthly: cost review per app × executor · idle-seat reclaim · ACU anomaly check. Quarterly: re-tier apps on the telemetry (a T3 app entering modernization becomes T1; a finished T1 drops back).
Campaign kill-switch: any Devin campaign whose cost per merged, verified subtask exceeds the Copilot-supervised alternative for two consecutive reviews is stopped — the playbook stays, the spend doesn't.

Sequence in one line

  • Index — Devin wikis for every app, owner-verified
  • Standardize — AGENTS.md + artifact templates everywhere
  • Tier — T1 campaign targets · T2 full flow · T3 wiki-only
  • Wire — Copilot floor + Devin campaign lane + one PR lane
  • Operate — telemetry, cadence, kill-switch

What this buys the portfolio

  • Stage 01 becomes near-free — comprehension is pre-built, not re-bought per ticket
  • Any engineer can enter any app — same wiki, same artifact shape, same gate
  • Devin spend is structurally contained — campaigns only, pooled, capped, piloted
  • Cross-app blast radius is visible — the portfolio dependency map catches what single-repo analysis misses

Honest caveats on this setup pattern

“DeepWiki-the-whole-estate-first” is a sound pattern with thin independent evidence — Cognition claims bank-scale indexing (400k+ repos) but publishes no accuracy figures, hence the mandatory owner-verification step. Wiki freshness lag, private-repo onboarding effort, and CI quality are the three practical blockers that stall this setup most often. Pricing signatures approximate — verify before rollout.

Assumptions · Run the Bank — state them or the routing is wrong

  • Work is interrupt-driven, not roadmap-driven — incidents, defects, small changes, compliance patches, vendor/platform upgrades. Arrival is unpredictable; queues, not sprints, are the honest unit.
  • Stability outranks throughput — the cost of a bad change (incident, regulatory breach) dwarfs the cost of a slow one. Blast-radius control is the design goal.
  • Time-to-safe-restore governs incidents — during an incident, no agent writes to production paths; AI accelerates diagnosis, humans execute the fix under change control.
  • Product's role is thin here — a product owner triages and prioritizes the queue; there is no spec-writing culture for BAU items and this flow doesn't pretend otherwise.
  • Ecosystem (Tab 2) is live — verified wikis, AGENTS.md, artifact templates, telemetry, and tiered app access already exist.
  • Team shape — a pooled support/BAU rotation plus app-aligned engineers; Devin access sits with 1–2 campaign leads, not the whole rotation.

RTB working agreement — Product × Engineering

Product owns the queue, not the tools — triage, priority, and customer comms. Tool routing is an engineering decision made by the split rule, invisible to the backlog.
Engineering protects the incident boundary — read-only AI during incidents, no exceptions; the emergency CR process is unchanged by any of this tooling.
The debt-bundle review is the shared ritual — monthly, 30 minutes, one output: which bundles graduate to the campaign lane this cycle, at what ACU cap.

RTB telemetry — what “good” looks like

MTTR trend (does wiki-assisted diagnosis actually shrink it?) · defect escape & reopen rate on Copilot-agent PRs · campaign cost per merged remediation vs. the queue alternative · zero unplanned Devin sessions — any interrupt-triggered ACU spend is a governance miss, not a productivity win.

Assumptions · Change the Bank — state them or the routing is wrong

  • Work is roadmap-driven and spec-able — features, journeys, integrations, modernization epics. Product can invest in specification before code, so the artifact discipline pays for itself.
  • Throughput-with-quality is the goal — cycle time matters, but rework and defect escape are the true cost drivers (METR's caution: AI can feel faster while being slower — measure it).
  • Two CTB species exist — new build (features on T2 product apps) and modernization (T1 legacy campaigns). They use the tools almost oppositely, so this tab splits them.
  • Product participates in artifacts — POs co-author specs and review plans at the gate; acceptance criteria are written to be machine-checkable wherever possible, because that's what unlocks the cheap lanes.
  • Ecosystem (Tab 2) is live — and T1 apps flagged for modernization have owner-verified wikis + playbook investment already made.
  • Funding follows mode — CTB carries the project budget; Devin ACU pools are a line item per modernization epic, not a shared utility.

The spec is now the product team's interface to the tooling. Every epic runs the full Tab-1 artifact chain — and the quality of acceptance criteria directly determines how much work qualifies for the cheap async lanes (coding agent) and the campaign lane (Devin).

CTB working agreement — Product × Engineering

Product writes acceptance criteria as if a machine will check them — because one will. Vague criteria silently tax the epic by forcing everything into the supervised lane.
Engineering owns lane classification at the gate — and re-classifies as specs firm up mid-epic; satellite work migrates to the async lane as criteria sharpen.
Modernization epics budget review capacity explicitly — parallel Devin sessions can out-produce human review; throttle sessions to review throughput, never the reverse.

CTB telemetry — what “good” looks like

Cost per merged PR by lane (supervised vs. async vs. campaign) · rework/churn rate per lane — the honest counterweight to velocity feel (METR) · % of epic scope flowing through async + campaign lanes as a spec-quality proxy · pilot pass-rate before scale on every campaign · review-queue depth as the throttle signal.
